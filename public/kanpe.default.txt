=== Why do you want to work at Anthropic? ===

My interest in Anthropic comes from a personal experience that changed how I see AI coding tools.

Last month, I spent about $100 on another AI coding service to build a personal software assistant. Even with its top-tier Astra Ultra model, I used up roughly five weeks of weekly usage limits, and the project could not finish a single full test run.

I then switched to Claude Code on the $20 plan, using Sonnet. In about two days, I had a working version. It was not a controlled benchmark, but what surprised me was that a cheaper model delivered what a higher-tier model could not. I was especially impressed by how accurately Sonnet understood my instructions and turned them into working software.

This experience convinced me that Anthropic has the potential to become the world's number one AI company.

At the same time, I am honest about my limits. When I build with AI coding tools, I often feel like a site supervisor who watches progress without fully understanding the implementation. I worry that as AI advances, even the world's top experts may end up in a similar position, like running a nuclear power plant with no one able to maintain it. That is why I believe safety measures are not optional, and why Anthropic's focus on reliable, interpretable, and steerable AI resonates with me.

This also showed me how important product reliability and good customer support are. I know firsthand how frustrating it is when technical problems stop users from reaching their goals.

My background includes technical support for ADSL modems and fiber routers in Japan, and work as a BPO agent supporting Uber in Malaysia, covering delivery drivers (billing and onboarding), restaurant partners, and Uber Eats customers. At Deloitte, I developed apps with Power Platform and Office Scripts, and created training apps and several training manuals.

As a Product Support Specialist, I would like to combine my hands-on support experience with my technical background in app development and documentation to help customers solve technical problems and get the most out of Anthropic's products.

I want to contribute to Anthropic's long-term success and grow alongside a company whose technology I genuinely believe in.

=== How do you handle a difficult customer? ===

First, I stay calm and I listen.
I do not argue. I say sorry for the trouble.
Then I ask for the details, like the order and the time.
I explain what I can do and what I cannot do.
I do not promise anything I cannot do.
If I cannot solve it, I escalate with the facts.

For example, at Uber Eats, a certain number of the calls started with shouting.
Sometimes the customer waited two or three hours.
I understood why they were angry.
I did not make excuses, and I explained clearly what I could do.
Most of the time, I took their anger and talked with them for one or two hours.
Then their anger calmed down, and we closed the case.


=== Can you tell me about a time you solved a customer's problem? ===

At Uber Eats, we had a "ghost customer" problem.
The driver picked up the food, but the eater's information disappeared.
It happened many times.
I felt that these orders looked suspicious.
Many of them were cash orders, and no payment came.
The eater names were also strange and hard to read.
So I told my team leader directly.
I said I thought they were bad-faith orders.
I asked the manager to decide how to handle them.
Then the support team agreed it was a prank.
We checked the data and force-closed the orders.
I learned that speaking up early about repeated problems helps the team.


=== Can you tell me about a time you failed? ===

At Uber Eats, one failure was that I could not support some delivery drivers who spoke neither Japanese nor English, for example people from countries like Vietnam, Kazakhstan, and Azerbaijan.
Our Japanese team was meant to support people who could communicate in Japanese or English.
At that time, internet use was restricted.
I really did not know what to do.
I asked my team leader for advice, but in the end I did not call them back.
Looking back, I think we should at least have shared this information with the client.


=== A customer says the Claude API is not working. What would you do? ===

First, I would say sorry for the trouble.
Then I would ask what they are trying to do.
Next, I would ask for the error message and the HTTP status code.
I would also ask for the request ID, the model name, and when it started.
Then I would try to reproduce the problem.
I would check the official docs and the status page.
If I find the cause, I would explain the fix in simple steps.
If I cannot solve it, I would escalate to engineers with all the details.
That way, the customer does not need to explain again.
After that, I would follow up and share what I learned with my team.
